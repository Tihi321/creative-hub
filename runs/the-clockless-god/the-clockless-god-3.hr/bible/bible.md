# Story bible (light)

## Setting
Present day, Monday 23 March to Thursday 2 April, with a fictional total solar eclipse over Paris on Wednesday 1 April, totality just before three in the afternoon. Chapters 1 to 3 in Paris: the Marais, the Île Saint-Louis, the police judiciaire, the quarries under Denfert-Rochereau. Chapters 4 to 5 and the first half of 6 in the fictional watchmaking village of Les Bréviaires on the snowbound Franches-Montagnes plateau of the Swiss Jura, reached by train via Basel and Delémont. Chapters 6 to 9 back in Paris, ending at the Panthéon. France's legal time comes from the ALS162 longwave signal from Allouis, which public clocks, traffic lights, stations and hospitals listen to. None, the ninth hour, is three in the afternoon. The Brotherhood marks everything with a clock face that has IX at the top.

## Style
A visual novel script, comic adventure-mystery: real clues, real danger, humour from the characters. Scenes alternate focus between Dec and Inès (odd source chapters Dec, even Inès), usually one or two scenes for each in every chapter. Each scene opens with SCENE: place and time, two or three short present-tense narration lines on what the reader sees, an ON SCREEN line naming at most three characters and how they look, then dialogue, one short line per text box, with expressions only from neutral, happy, sad, angry, surprised, worried, scared, thinking. Physical clues are shown on screen before the heroes use them. Keep the source's jokes and running gags (Dec's French, Pichon's lunches, 'not partners', Kessler and animals). The Brotherhood is genuinely sinister even when the heroes are funny. Never wink at the reader, no em dashes, no morals. Each chapter ends on a turn or a find. About 1,600 to 1,800 words per chapter.
